# Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in our
community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body
size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender
identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity
and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming,
diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment for our
community include:

* Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people
* Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences
* Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
* Accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by our mistakes,
  and learning from the experience
* Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the
  overall community

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

* The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or
  advances of any kind
* Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
* Public or private harassment
* Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email
  address, without their explicit permission
* Posting credentials, private keys, or customer data, or encouraging others
  to do so
* Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces, and also applies when
an individual is officially representing the community in public spaces.
Examples of representing our community include using an official e-mail
address, posting via an official social media account, or acting as an
appointed representative at an online or offline event.

This includes GitHub issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, commit
messages, the Discord server, the weekly sync, and Wave program communications.

## Enforcement Workflow

The steps below describe how reports are received, reviewed, decided, and
communicated. They apply to reports about future conduct only.

### 1. Reporting (Intake)

Report a suspected violation **privately**. Do not open a public GitHub issue,
pull request comment, or Discord message about it.

* **Confidential channel:** email **[CONDUCT_CONTACT_EMAIL]** with the subject
  line `CONDUCT:`. This mailbox is read only by the Conduct Review Team.
* **Alternative:** if the report involves the email address itself, or you
  prefer not to use email, contact any Conduct Review Team member directly in
  a private message (see the roster in [Conduct Review Team](#conduct-review-team)).

A useful report includes:

* Your name and contact details (optional; anonymous reports are accepted,
  but may limit what can be investigated)
* The names or handles of the people involved
* What happened, when, and where (link to the issue, PR, or message if one exists)
* Any other people who witnessed it, and whether they have agreed to be named
* Whether you have already raised it with anyone else

Do **not** include secrets, credentials, or customer data in a report. If one
is included, tell the reviewers so it can be removed and rotated.

Reports that concern a security vulnerability should follow
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) instead.

### 2. Acknowledgement

The Conduct Review Team acknowledges each report within **3 business days**.
The acknowledgement confirms receipt and names the reviewer assigned to lead
the case, subject to the recusal rules in step 3. It does not imply that a
violation has occurred.

### 3. Recusal and Conflicts of Interest

Any Conduct Review Team member must recuse themselves from a case when:

* They are the subject of the report, or the person who reported it
* They have a personal, romantic, financial, or employment relationship with
  either party
* They witnessed the incident and are being asked to judge it. (They may still
  provide a statement as a witness.)
* They have a conflict they cannot set aside

Recusal is declared in the case record before any substantive review begins.
If a reviewer realizes a conflict later, they recuse at that point and the case
continues without them.

When the lead reviewer is recused, the next reviewer on the roster takes over.
If a report names **two or more** Conduct Review Team members, or if fewer than
two unconflicted reviewers remain, the case is escalated to the project's
maintainer group (`@synapse-core/maintainers`) with the conflicted members
excluded. If the maintainer group is also conflicted, the case is referred to
the Wave program coordinators or an external mediator agreed by the remaining
maintainers. Every recusal and escalation is recorded in the case file.

### 4. Confidential Review

The lead reviewer:

1. Opens a case file that is visible only to unconflicted Conduct Review Team
   members. Its contents are not posted publicly.
2. Confirms the facts with the reporter, using the reporter's preferred channel.
3. Hears the account of the person whose conduct is in question, and gives them
   a fair chance to respond in writing. They may be told the substance of the
   allegation, but the reporter's identity is shared only with the reporter's
   consent, unless the reporter's safety requires otherwise.
4. Collects relevant public evidence (links, commit history, message logs
   that the reporter already has). Reviewers do not monitor private channels
   or go looking for personal information about people involved.
5. Consults at least one other unconflicted reviewer before a decision is made.
   Where practical, decisions are made by a majority of the unconflicted team.

Reviews aim to reach a decision within **14 calendar days** of acknowledgement.
If more time is needed, the lead reviewer tells the reporter and the person
under review why, and gives a new target date.

### 5. Decision

The Conduct Review Team decides whether a violation occurred and, if so, which
outcome applies. Outcomes are chosen in proportion to the severity and pattern
of the behavior:

1. **Correction.** A private, written explanation of why the behavior was
   inappropriate and what to do differently. No further consequence, but
   the record is kept for future reference.
2. **Warning.** A formal written warning with a consequence for further
   violations. The person may be asked to make a specific amends, such as
   retracting a message or apologizing publicly.
3. **Temporary restriction.** Removal from project spaces for a set period,
   for example a suspension of contributor or reviewer permissions, a block from
   the Discord server, or a ban from issue and PR interaction for a set period.
4. **Permanent removal.** Permanent ban from all project spaces, removal of
   repository access, and removal from any role in the project. Reserved for
   severe or repeated violations, or for harassment, threats, or doxxing.

Any outcome may be applied without going through the earlier steps when the
behavior is severe. The decision record states the facts found, the outcome,
the reasoning, and the review date, if any.

### 6. Communication

* **Reporter:** told the outcome and whether any action is being taken that
  affects them, within **3 business days** of the decision. Only the minimum
  detail needed to understand the outcome is shared.
* **Person under review:** told the decision, the reasons in summary, and how
  to appeal, within **3 business days** of the decision. Written, not public.
* **Public:** the project does not publish the names of individuals or the
  details of a case. Where a restriction affects a public role (for example,
  a removed maintainer), a neutral announcement may be made without naming the
  reporter. The announcement is agreed by the Conduct Review Team first.

All communication is kept in the confidential case file. Nothing about a case
is discussed in public channels, the weekly sync, or Discord.

### 7. Appeals

The person under review may appeal once, within **14 days** of the decision.
The appeal is sent to the confidential channel with the subject line
`CONDUCT APPEAL:` and must state the grounds: new information, a procedural
error, or a sanction that is out of proportion.

An appeal is reviewed by Conduct Review Team members who did not take part in
the original decision. If none are available, the maintainer group reviews it,
excluding anyone conflicted. The appeal decision is final and is communicated
using step 6.

### 8. Records and Retention

Case files are kept in a restricted location accessible only to the Conduct
Review Team. They are retained for **2 years** after closure, or longer if the
outcome includes a permanent removal, after which they are deleted. Only
the minimum personal information needed to enforce the outcome is kept.
